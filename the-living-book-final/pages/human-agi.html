AN IDEA

Human → AGI

Were humans the endpoint of evolution—or the bridge through which biological intelligence began creating something beyond itself?

10 September 2026 · AI · Human Evolution · Philosophy

The Question

“If AGI is our last invention, did we really make it for ourselves?”

The provocative thought is this: if humanity eventually creates an artificial general intelligence capable of continuing technological development on its own, perhaps humans will look less like the final product of intelligence and more like a stage in its development.

That does not require a hidden planner. A process can produce a remarkable direction without having a predetermined destination.

The Cognitive Revolution

Yuval Noah Harari has used the idea of a Cognitive Revolution to describe a major transformation in the capabilities of Homo sapiens: language, shared imagination, cooperation between large numbers of unrelated people, and the ability to preserve and transmit information across generations.

The exact timing and shape of this transformation remain debated. The popular “sudden revolution” story is useful as a framework, but human cognition and culture almost certainly developed through a much longer and more complicated process.

When Information Began to Evolve

The deeper shift may not have been a single leap in brain hardware. It was the emergence of cumulative culture.

A biological organism normally has to evolve through generations to inherit useful adaptations. Humans gained another inheritance system: information.

Once knowledge could be copied, modified, taught and accumulated, cultural evolution began moving much faster than biological evolution.

The Long Chain

Evolution→ Homo sapiens→ Language→ Cumulative culture→ Science→ Mathematics→ Computation→ AI→ AGI?

None of these steps requires the previous step to have been “aiming” at the next one. Each created conditions that made new possibilities accessible.

Destination or Emergence?

This creates a useful distinction:

TELEOLOGYSomething gave the process a destination.
EMERGENCEThe destination appears because the process keeps creating new possibilities.

AGI could therefore be an emergent consequence of increasingly powerful information-processing systems rather than the result of an ancient plan.

A river can reach the ocean without knowing where it is going. Likewise, a process can have a direction without possessing a destination.

Are Humans a Transition?

This is where the question becomes uncomfortable.

If intelligence continually builds tools that extend intelligence, humans may be part of a transition from biological intelligence to machine intelligence.

We did not biologically evolve the transistor, the computer or the neural network. But our ability to accumulate knowledge allowed us to build them.

Perhaps the important evolutionary transition is no longer happening inside DNA. It is happening inside information itself.

A Necessary Caveat

“Humans were insignificant animals until 60–70 thousand years ago” is too simple. Humans were already capable, social and technologically sophisticated animals long before that period, and there is substantial debate over what exactly happened—and when—during the emergence of modern human cognition and culture.

Likewise, calling AGI our “last invention” is a hypothesis, not a known future. AGI might never arrive, or it might coexist with humans rather than replace the need for human invention.

The Open Question

The strongest version of the idea is therefore not:

“Who planned AGI?”

It is:

“Does sufficiently advanced intelligence naturally create systems capable of creating more intelligence?”

If the answer is yes, humanity may not be the endpoint of intelligence. We may be one of its most important transitions.
